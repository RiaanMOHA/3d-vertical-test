## Architecture and design notes

### Floor architecture rule (saved as memory `feedback_floor_architecture.md`)

**Rule:** Each room owns its own floor, sized exactly to its footprint. No full-footprint base layer. Everyone sits at y=0; only genkan steps down to y=-0.15.

**Why:** the LDK-base + 1 mm overlay pattern z-fought from top-down view at orbit distance (~15 m camera). The 1 mm gap is at depth-buffer precision edge; LDK wood texture flickers through wet-room flat colors. User confirmed the fix works on 1F: "1F fixed, SO KEEP IT LIKE THAT NOW".

**How to apply:**
1. New room → exact footprint, no overlap with neighbors.
2. Same-material adjacent rooms can be merged into a single rectangle when their union is rectangular (the 2F closet merges from prior session).
3. Different-material adjacent rooms abut at a shared edge.
4. Hairline gaps at boundaries → fix with thin saddle-bar threshold strips on top (the 2F 30 mm × 3 mm `matThreshold` 見切り材 pattern), NOT a base layer.
5. Never use polygonOffset, 1 mm step, or layerOnTop to resolve coplanar floor conflict. Decompose into non-overlapping rectangles.

### Mesh-merge pattern for L-shapes

THREE.js doesn't have a direct "make L-shape from two rectangles" primitive. Two approaches:

1. **THREE.Shape + THREE.ExtrudeGeometry:** define the L-shape as a 2D polygon, extrude by FLOOR_T. Material applied per-shape. Vertices are explicit; no GPU sub-pixel ambiguity at the L's interior corner.
2. **BufferGeometry merge:** create separate BoxGeometries for each rectangle, merge via `BufferGeometryUtils.mergeBufferGeometries()`. Single draw call, but the original rectangle edges still exist in the merged buffer (just in one mesh).

Approach 1 eliminates the seam entirely (the L is one continuous polygon). Approach 2 reduces draw calls but preserves the seam at the merge point.

For the user's mandate (no gap-causing GPU artifacts), **Approach 1 is correct**. Pattern is reusable for both the corridor merge and the closet merge.
