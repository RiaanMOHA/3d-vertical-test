## Problems encountered and how they were solved

### Problem 1: Multiple wall-position drifts surfaced after washroom z1 fix

- **Symptom.** Moving washroom from z=4.80 to z=5.40 created a 0.90 m strip of corridor-like space at `x=1.80..3.60, z=4.50..5.40` that had no collision box. Plus the bath south wall was still at z=4.80 (didn't move with the washroom box).
- **Root cause.** The collision boxes (rooms array) and the wall geometry (wallX/wallZ calls) were being treated as independent systems. Phase C corrected the washroom box but left the wall geometry stale. This is the second time a Phase C correction has been incomplete — pre-existing geometry has not been audited against Phase C.
- **Fix.** Found the strip's actual content via 400-dpi blueprint render at `/tmp/ozu-1f-band.png` (showed bath door swing arc into hallway space), then added corridor-1 box. Also moved bath south wall from z=4.80 to z=5.40 in Phase 3.
- **Lesson.** When fixing room dimensions, ALSO audit the wall geometry that bounds those rooms. The collision-box system (rooms array) and the wall-rendering system (wallX/wallZ calls) need to stay in sync.

### Problem 2: Iteration order matters for collision-box labels

- **Symptom.** Walking in the kitchen showed `living-dining` instead of `kitchen` — even though kitchen is a separate carve-out at `x=0..2.55, z=2.40..4.50`.
- **Root cause.** The label-lookup in `setRoomLabel` returns on first match, iterating the rooms array in order. The LDK bbox `x=0..6.30, z=0..4.50` covers the kitchen carve-out without exclusion. Same issue for 1F stair shaft within LDK bbox.
- **Fix.** Reordered the rooms array so carve-outs (kitchen, 1F stairs) come BEFORE living-dining. Also added comment marker on the array explaining the ordering requirement.
- **Lesson.** First-match wins. Always list carve-outs before their parent bbox in the rooms array.

### Problem 3: Stairs box used 1F footprint on both floors

- **Symptom.** At 2F, room-4's SE corner mislabeled as "stairs"; 2F shaft west portion uncovered.
- **Root cause.** Single `stairs` box with `y=0..5.0` (both floors) used the 1F shaft footprint `x=5.40..6.30, z=2.90..4.50`. But the L-bend means 2F shaft is at `x=4.725..6.30, z=3.60..4.50` (turns west going up). Different footprint.
- **Fix.** Split `stairs` into two boxes: 1F at `y=0..2.5` with 1F footprint, 2F at `y=2.5..5.0` with 2F footprint.
- **Lesson.** L-shape stairs need separate 1F + 2F collision boxes since the shaft footprint changes at the L-bend.

### Problem 4: Two interior scenes differed wildly in window/door positions

- **Symptom.** User showed 2 screenshots of building front view, both rendered from `ozu-test.html`, but with completely different appearance — one brown/cream cladded with proper porch, one all-white with random small windows scattered.
- **Root cause.** The exterior scene (`registerScene('exterior', ...)`) and the interior scene (`registerScene('interior', ...)`) had INDEPENDENT outer wall geometry. They were built separately at different times. Window/door positions, sizes, and materials all differed. The "all-white" screenshot was the interior scene viewed from outside (its outer walls use `wallMat` plain material since the interior scene is meant to be viewed from inside).
- **Fix.** Made the exterior scene use the SAME window arrays (F1_WIN_*, F2_WIN_*) as the interior scene. Refactored makeWall/wallX/wallZ to accept optional material so the interior scene's outer walls can render with cladding when viewed from outside.
- **Lesson.** Two scenes that depict the same physical building MUST share window/door position data, otherwise they will drift over time. Future structural changes to the building should update one source-of-truth array (the F1_WIN/F2_WIN constants) and both scenes will pick it up automatically.

### Problem 5: Interior wall outer cladding makes wet-room interiors brown

- **Symptom.** Applying `matLowerExt` (brown panel) to the front 1F LEFT wall covers BOTH faces of that wall. Inside the wet rooms (laundry, bath, toilet), the front exterior wall now shows brown instead of plaster.
- **Root cause.** Three.js `BoxGeometry` with single material applies that material to all 6 faces. To have different mats on +Z (outside) vs -Z (inside) requires either (a) per-face material array on the box or (b) two thin walls stacked back-to-back.
- **Fix.** Not yet applied. User asked to do `/handoff` first. Two options remain: per-face material array, or 2-layer wall stack.
- **Lesson.** When walls have visually different inside/outside surfaces, single-material walls don't work. Decide upfront: either accept the cosmetic compromise, or build 2-face walls from the start.

### F1_WIN/F2_WIN arrays are the canonical window source

The interior scene has F1_WIN_FRONT, F1_WIN_BACK, F1_WIN_LEFT, F2_WIN_FRONT, F2_WIN_BACK, F2_WIN_LEFT constants (`ozu-test.html:822-833, 881-893`). These are now the canonical source for window positions across both scenes. Each entry is `{ a, b, y0, y1 }` with the Japanese window code in a comment. All position changes should update these arrays.

### Each room has ONE entrance door per blueprint

The blueprint convention: each bedroom has exactly ONE entrance door (on the entrance-wall facing the corridor) and one closet door (bi-fold, on the closet-wall facing the central column closet). The "walk-through closet" routing for room-3 (where the room reached corridor through an east closet) was a non-blueprint hack and has been removed.

### The exterior cladding split is at x=4.95, not x=4.50

When looking at the photos, the brown panel ends roughly half-way across the front. I initially set the split at code-x=4.50 (the genkan's interior boundary). But on closer inspection the boundary in the photo is at the door's left edge, which is code-x=4.95. Keeping it at 4.95 also avoids subdividing the existing wall segment at 4.23..4.95 (which is fully brown).

### The all-white interior scene render has been a long-standing visual bug

The interior scene's `wallMat` is a single warm-grey material used for ALL walls (interior partitions + outer walls). When you orbit the interior scene from outside, you see this single material on the outer walls, which doesn't match the cladded exterior scene. This wasn't a bug introduced this session — it's been there since the interior scene was registered. The user finally noticed it when they took screenshots of two scenes side-by-side. Fix: matLowerExt/matUpperExt now applied to outer walls in the interior scene.

## Architecture and design notes

### Window position single-source-of-truth

The F1_WIN_FRONT, F1_WIN_BACK, F1_WIN_LEFT, F2_WIN_FRONT, F2_WIN_BACK, F2_WIN_LEFT arrays in the interior scene's build function (`ozu-test.html:822-833, 881-893`) are now the canonical source for window positions. Both the exterior scene (lines 313-425) and the interior scene's outer walls (lines 826-836, 895-899) reference these arrays. The exterior scene maps `F1_WIN_FRONT.map(w => [w.a, w.b])` etc to wall gaps and `paneFront(w.a, w.b, w.y0, w.y1, D)` to glass panes. Interior scene does the same via `wallX/wallZ` with `.map(...)` of gap arrays.

This means: to add/move/remove a window, edit ONE array. Both scenes update.

### Cladding application strategy (current vs ideal)

Current approach: outer walls are SINGLE-MATERIAL boxes. The whole wall (both faces) gets `matUpperExt` (cream) or `matLowerExt` (brown). This means brown panel is visible from inside the wet rooms.

Ideal approach: 2-face walls. Either (a) THREE.BoxGeometry with material array `[mat0, mat1, mat2, mat3, mat4, mat5]` for the 6 box faces (right/left/top/bottom/front/back), with cladding on the outside face and `wallMat` on the inside face; or (b) two thin BoxGeometries stacked back-to-back with their own materials. (a) is more efficient (1 mesh per wall segment); (b) is simpler to implement.

This is the "two-layer wall fix" the user asked me to consider. Pending decision.

### Why the front cladding split is at x=4.95, not x=4.50

The genkan's interior boundary (per blueprint) is at code-x=4.50 (page-x=1800), which is also the bath/toilet east edge. But the photo's brown-cream visual boundary is at the LEFT edge of the entry door — code-x=4.95 (page-x=1350). I'm matching the photo's visual boundary. Bonus: avoids subdividing the wall segment at x=4.23..4.95 (which is fully brown anyway).

### Iteration order for collision boxes

`setRoomLabel` returns on FIRST match in the rooms array. So order matters. Convention now established: list carve-outs (kitchen, stairs) BEFORE their parent bbox (LDK). The 2F section also has stairs listed before the bedrooms.
