## Learnings and discoveries

### `polygonOffset` is a debugging trap

`polygonOffset` is the right tool for one specific problem: suppressing z-fight where two coplanar surfaces in the same model would otherwise flicker. But the offset applies to *every* fragment that material renders, not just at the intended z-fight surfaces. If the mesh using polygonOffset is anywhere near another mesh's surface — even at an unrelated intersection — the offset can shift the depth value enough to make the offset-material win the z-test against the other surface.

In this codebase:
- `trimMat` had polygonOffset to suppress trim-wall z-fight (trim sits flush with wall inner face).
- The same `trimMat` is used for bedroom baseboards (which extend from y=2.5 to y=2.57 — pierce the 2F floor at y=2.503..2.505) and 2F door jambs (extend y=2.5 to y=4.57 — also pierce the floor).
- At every intersection of trim with the 2F floor, the polygonOffset biased the trim's depth toward the camera enough to win the z-test → trim color bled through the floor.

**The trade-off if polygonOffset is removed:** From-inside-room views may have wall z-fight at the trim's outer face (coplanar with wall inner face). Empirically the user reports the floor view is now clean; they have not reported any wall flicker. Future sessions should keep an eye on the inside-room view of bedrooms — if wall-trim flickering becomes visible, the right fix is to *reposition the trim 1 mm into the room* (offset `stripC` by an extra 0.001) rather than re-adding polygonOffset.

### Trust empirical data over theory

The user's "DEBUG=true → no lines / DEBUG=false → lines" observation isolated the cause to whatever's different between DEBUG modes. I had created the dbg materials as separate `MeshStandardMaterial` instances *without* polygonOffset, falling back to `trimMat` (with polygonOffset) in production mode. The single load-bearing difference between modes was polygonOffset. I should have spotted this *immediately* once the user's data came in — instead I wasted four more rounds of geometric coordinate tweaks based on incomplete theories.

The right move: when DEBUG-vs-production gives a clean before/after, *diff the DEBUG vs production code paths first*, not the *geometry*.

## Architecture and design notes

### The polygonOffset trade-off

`trimMat` previously had `polygonOffset: true, polygonOffsetFactor: -1, polygonOffsetUnits: -1`. This was added to suppress z-fight where the trim's outer face is coplanar with the wall's inner face (the trim sits flush against the wall by design). Removing polygonOffset means that coplanar surface is no longer protected, and from inside a bedroom looking at the wall-trim junction, the GPU may coin-flip at coplanar pixels → wall flickering through trim.

**However:** the user prioritized killing the floor lines over hypothetical wall flickering. As of 2026-05-11, no wall flickering has been reported.

If wall flickering re-emerges, the right fix is **not** to re-add polygonOffset. The right fix is to:
1. Add a 1 mm offset to the trim position so it's no longer coplanar with the wall: `stripC += sign * 0.001` in `addRoomTrim` and `addWallTrim`. Trim outer face moves from wall inner face (wall_center + WT/2) to wall_inner_face + 1mm.
2. From inside the room, the 1 mm offset is invisible at viewing distance — the trim still reads as flush with the wall.
3. From any view angle, the trim is no longer coplanar with the wall → no z-fight → no polygonOffset needed.

### The 2F floor layering

Final state of the 2F floor architecture:
- **Floor top face:** y = F1H + F2_LIFT = 2.5 + 0.005 = **2.505 m**
- **Floor bottom face:** y = 2.505 - F2_FLOOR_T = 2.505 - 0.002 = **2.503 m**
- **Floor thickness:** 2 mm (thin slab — visible thickness at room edges is invisible from any normal viewing angle)
- **All 1F surfaces (walls, ceiling, crown molding) below:** y ≤ 2.500 m, with the LDK / 1F corridor crown specifically at y ≤ 2.495 (dropped 5 mm)
- **Threshold saddle bars:** sit on top of the floor at y = F1H + F2_LIFT + TH/2 = 2.5065 m

This architecture survives even if `polygonOffset` was somehow re-added to `trimMat`: the 8 mm gap between the LDK crown top (2.495) and the floor top (2.505) is large enough that polygonOffset depth bias couldn't overcome it.

### Per-use-site debug palette pattern

The 5 dbg materials (`matBaseDbg`, `matCrownDbg`, `matJambDbg`, `matHeaderDbg`, `matLdkTrimDbg`) collapse to `trimMat` when `DEBUG_2F_LINES = false`. This is a useful pattern for future debugging passes:

```js
const matFooDbg = DEBUG_FLAG ? new THREE.MeshStandardMaterial({ color: 0x..., roughness: 0.8 }) : trimMat;
```

The pattern lets you isolate one use-site of a shared material without permanent code duplication. Setting DEBUG_FLAG=true gives each use-site its own color in a single screenshot.
