### Lighting iterations on laundry-test

**Iteration 1 — initial setup (before research):** warm `0xfff0d8` sun-through-window, DirectionalLight at intensity 1.28, exposure 0.52, env intensity 0.65. Read as "evening / golden hour" not "morning" — wrong vibe.

**Iteration 2 — first quality pass:** bumped exposure to 0.82, env intensity to 0.65, frosted glass to transmission 0.80 / roughness 0.55 / envMapIntensity 0.85, added scene.environmentRotation, ceiling spotlights dropped to 0.7 each. **Result:** washer started to feel too bright; user complained "lighting almost there but not quite".

**Iteration 3 — May 2026 cutting-edge research (4 parallel agents):**
- Agent 1 (Cycles bake May 2026): Light Portal + Sky Texture Nishita + Translucent BSDF glass swap during bake, 1024² × 512 samples + OIDN
- Agent 2 (three.js r184 May 2026): `scene.environmentRotation` for HDRI sun alignment, frosted-glass best-practice values, RectAreaLight LTC differs WebGL vs WebGPU
- Agent 3 (real-time daylight): stick with baked Combined lightmap + IBL + SSGI top-up — current best practice
- Agent 4 (frosted glass): roughness bimodal (0.05-0.15 or 0.65-0.85) — 0.55 was noisy middle. Sky proxy must be HDR-bright. Backlit-glow trick = envMapIntensity 2.5 on glass + UnrealBloomPass

Applied: frosted glass roughness 0.78, transmission 1.0, attenuation tint, envMapIntensity 2.5, procedural micro-roughness + faint normalMap, UnrealBloomPass added via EffectComposer, bake script rewritten for Light Portal + Sky Texture Nishita. **Result:** washer STILL too bright.

**Iteration 4 — user enforced Kumamoto-11AM rule:** "All interior renders = Kumamoto / sunny spring / 11 AM JST". Saved to CLAUDE.md + memory. Re-tuned everything:
- Sun position: azimuth 160° (SSE), elevation 60°
- Color temp: ~5500 K neutral-cool (NEVER warm-yellow)
- North window (laundry's window): NO DIRECT SUN — diffuse cool sky only
- Dropped exposure 0.82 → 0.62, env intensity 0.65 → 0.45, hemisphere 0.48 → 0.36, RectAreaLight 26.0 → 14.0, sky proxy cool, sun DirectionalLight REMOVED entirely

**Result:** "Window blocked by something" — over-corrected, scene too dim, all crisp shadows gone.

**Iteration 5 — bring brightness back, keep rule:** exposure 0.62 → 0.78, env intensity 0.45 → 0.60, hemisphere 0.36 → 0.55, RectAreaLight 14 → 22, sky proxy HDR-bright via `color.multiplyScalar(3.2)` + `toneMapped: false`, glass roughness 0.78 → 0.62. **Result:** window visible again BUT no shadows.

**Iteration 6 — restore shadow-caster:** added new `winSky` DirectionalLight (cool color `0xe0e8f4`, intensity 0.95, shadow.radius 10 for frosted-soft edges). Updated CLAUDE.md rule + memory: north windows STILL need a directional shadow-caster — it represents the directional component of sky-bounce. **Result:** shadows restored, window cool, washer materials dimmed separately (envInt 0.35 white / 0.45 chrome).

**Iteration 7 — final dim:** "still a bit too bright". Exposure 0.78 → 0.68, sky proxy 3.2× → 2.4×, bloom 0.22 → 0.16. **Final state:** user said "we are done with this room".

Living-dining is the OPPOSITE pattern — the ROOM is the deliverable, with the TV (and future appliances) as featured items. Don't repeat the laundry mistake of adding scope creep, but DO build the full room since that's the explicit scope of `*-test` files per `feedback_test_suffix_means_full_reconstruction.md`.

### Garden-wall is THE lighting feature of living-dining

The DirectionalLight at azimuth 160° / elevation 60° actually streams into the room here, with crisp cast-shadows on the floor. This is the OPPOSITE of laundry-test (north window, no direct sun) — don't reuse laundry-test's cool-blue values verbatim.
