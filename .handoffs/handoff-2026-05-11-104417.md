### Problem 1: GLTFLoader import broke whole script (catastrophic)

- **Symptom:** Entire canvas dark on Exterior load. Chips visible (DOM), no 3D rendering.
- **Root cause:** GLTFLoader.js imports ~75 named symbols from `'three'` (Bone, AnimationClip, FileLoader, Loader, Box3, ImageBitmapLoader, InstancedMesh, Group, …). The vendored `three.module.min.js` is the minimal WebGL core bundle and exports only ~200 named entries — many GLTFLoader-required ones are not included. ESM import resolution failed silently and halted the entire `<script type="module">`.
- **Fix:** Reverted the GLTFLoader import + the runtime `new GLTFLoader().load(...)` block. Restored procedural wrinkled-plane sheet. Left a comment block in the code documenting the parked state and the path forward.
- **Lesson:** Before adding a new vendored example-module import, verify the project's vendored three bundle re-exports everything the module needs. Cheap check: `grep -oE '[A-Z][a-zA-Z0-9_]+' <module.js> | sort -u` against the bundle's exports. For r184, `three.module.min.js` is the WebGL-only minimal surface; `three.webgpu.js` (non-min) exports the full surface and would support GLTFLoader.

### Problem 2: Cloth-sim bake had mismatched UVs vs runtime mesh

- **Symptom:** Bake produced visible PNG outputs but the wrinkle detail was confined to ~25% of the texture (a T-shape region in `sheet_ao.png`). The remainder was empty white.
- **Root cause:** `bpy.ops.uv.smart_project` was called on the cloth-simmed sheet, repacking its UVs into a smaller island. The runtime three.js mesh used `PlaneGeometry` default planar UVs (0..1 across the surface).
- **Fix attempt:** Removed `smart_project`. Default UVs from `primitive_plane_add` are planar and survive cloth sim. Re-baked; got better UV coverage.
- **Deeper problem:** Even with matching planar UVs, the Blender cloth-simmed mesh has vertices in *different geometric positions* than the runtime procedural mesh. UVs attach to vertices; same UV → different geometry. So baked normal/AO applied to the runtime mesh's UVs would land in flat regions and look wrong.
- **Correct fix:** glTF mesh-export pipeline — load the Blender mesh + the textures into the runtime together. Wrote it, but the GLTFLoader import broke the script (Problem 1). Pipeline is parked.

### Problem 3: Sheet overhang clipped iron-frame bedposts

- **Symptom:** Sheet visibly passing through the corner posts of the bed in the user screenshot.
- **Root cause:** Sheet was sized as `(mattress + 10 cm)` in both x and z directions for drape. The headboard corner posts at `x = 2.62..2.67, z ∈ {1.7, 2.67}` and the footboard at `x = 0.70..0.75, z ∈ {1.7, 2.67}` occupy the mattress short-side corners.
- **Fix:** Sheet sized to exact mattress footprint (`sheetW = mBox.x2 - mBox.x1`, `sheetD = mBox.z2 - mBox.z1`). No overhang. The iron frame blocks drape on three sides anyway (foot, head, ac-wall); the open south side could drape but per photo evidence the sheet is tucked.
- **Lesson:** When a mesh sits inside a structured frame (bed, cabinet, drawer well), default to exact-footprint sizing. Overhangs need to be explicitly validated against the surrounding geometry, not just added for "natural drape".

### Problem 4: Curtain hung through the mattress

- **Symptom:** Far-side curtain bunch (the south column at `z = 1.64`) visibly passed through the bed's south edge.
- **Root cause:** Bunch hung from `y = 1.85` (curtain rod) to `y = 0.10` (floor). The mattress is at `y = 0.10..0.50, z = 1.70..2.67, x = 0.70..2.67`. At the curtain's `x = 2.60`, the mattress is present. The curtain bunch has radius up to 0.08 m → effective z-range 1.56..1.72, which overlaps the mattress at z = 1.70..1.72 for `y < 0.50`. So the curtain bottom passed through the south edge of the mattress.
- **Fix:** Raised the far-side bunch hem from `y = 0.10` to `y = 0.55` (just above mattress top). Real photos show JP cafe-style curtains often end at the window sill, not the floor, so this is also photo-correct.
- **Lesson:** Curtain hems should respect the furniture footprint in front of them. Default to "above the highest furniture surface in that x/z column" instead of "floor".

### Problem 5: Inner sheer plane sliced through the desk

- **Symptom:** Discovered during Problem 4 diagnosis. The white sheer plane added in Phase 2 spanned floor-to-ceiling (`y = 0.025..1.825`) and cut horizontally across the room from z = 0.36 to z = 1.64. The desk at `x = 2.1..2.7, z = 0.4..1.4, y < 0.75` was directly in the sheer's path at x = 2.56.
- **Root cause:** Built the sheer as a full-wall curtain instead of recognizing it's a *window covering* — only the window opening's footprint should be filled.
- **Fix:** Cropped sheer to `y = 0.80..1.85` and `z = 0.405..1.595` (the window opening with a small overhang). No more intersection with anything below the window sill.
- **Lesson:** When adding a window covering, derive its bounds from the window opening's coordinates, not from the wall the window sits in.

### Problem 6: Desk drawer's x-dimension was swapped with z-dimension

- **Symptom:** Drawer body visibly extended past the desk and intersected the chair back.
- **Root cause:** `RoundedBoxGeometry(d * 0.85, drwH, drwW, ...)` — `d` is the desk's z-extent (1.0 m), used incorrectly as the body's x-size. The actual desk depth in x is `w = 0.6`. So the drawer body was 0.85 m wide in x, extending 12.5 cm past each side of the 0.60 m-deep desk. With center at `cx = 2.4`, it spanned x = 1.975..2.825, overlapping the chair back at x ≈ 2.025..2.095.
- **Fix:** Renamed the dimensions for clarity: `drwBodyDepth = w * 0.55` (0.33 m, into-desk along x), `drwW = d * 0.70` (0.70 m, along desk's z-width). Repositioned body center at `f.x1 + drwT + drwBodyDepth / 2 = 2.283` so the body sits ENTIRELY within the desk's x range.
- **Lesson:** When a geometry helper takes (width, height, depth) and the project's coordinate system has the wall on x and the desk along z, ALWAYS sanity-check which project axis maps to which geometry axis. Use named locals (`drwBodyDepth`, `drwW`) rather than reusing `w` and `d` for ambiguous meanings.

## Learnings and discoveries

### Room-1 pipeline reached "high-quality" state via a specific stack

Documented in full in the **Architecture and design notes** section below. The short version: WebGL renderer + r184 + MSAA + HDRI from Poly Haven + per-wall baked AO + procedural wood ceiling + PBR floor + flat-paint walls + MeshPhysicalMaterial with sheen/transmission/clearcoat for cloth and glass + DirectionalLight (for shadows) + RectAreaLight (for window-shaped soft light) + fill DirectionalLight + AmbientLight + 4 PointLights at pendants + additive vertex-colour god-ray cone. **Walls intentionally use `MeshBasicMaterial` with `toneMapped: false` so the painted color renders LITERAL (not light-direction tinted); AO maps provide corner darkening via material.map multiply.** This combination is the key to the room reading as "painted modern interior" not "lit 3D mesh".

### r184 MeshPhysicalMaterial.sheen replaces the manual onBeforeCompile fresnel patch

The room-1 codebase still has `applyClothShader` (a manual `onBeforeCompile` fresnel shader) and uses it for curtains. For the bedding rewrite in this session, switching to native `sheen` + `sheenColor` + `sheenRoughness` on `MeshPhysicalMaterial` gave cleaner cloth edge highlights without the shader patch. Available on r155+; r184 has it natively. Recommended: future cloth additions should use the native sheen, and consider migrating the curtain material in a future pass.

### The vendored three bundle is a minimal subset

`vendor/three/build/three.module.min.js` (~365 KB) is the **WebGL core only**. It does NOT re-export many symbols the example modules need: Bone, AnimationClip, FileLoader, Loader, ImageBitmapLoader, InstancedMesh, Group, Box3, …. To use GLTFLoader (or most other `examples/jsm/` modules beyond HDRLoader/RoundedBoxGeometry), the project must either:
1. Swap `three.module.min.js` for the full unminified `three.module.js` (also in vendor at `vendor/three/build/three.module.js`, 649 KB) — but that's also a minimal subset, doesn't include AnimationClip etc.
2. Swap for `three.webgpu.js` (the full r184 surface including all loaders, all materials, all geometry helpers, ~2.5 MB) — but the renderer currently uses `WebGLRenderer` so this might not all work.
3. Vendor a fuller WebGL bundle (e.g. build a custom three rollup or use the `three.module.js` from the npm dist, which exports more symbols).

The cleanest path for GLTFLoader specifically: copy `three.webgpu.js` and verify `WebGLRenderer` is still exported there.

### The A6000 bake pipeline works end-to-end

For object passes that produce 2D texture outputs (wall AO, procedural plush dimples baked to normal map, color variation, etc.), the existing pattern from `bake_room1_ao.py` + the new `bake_room1_bedding.py` is reliable:
- `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 ~/blender/blender-5.1.1-linux-x64/blender --background --python <script>`
- Cycles + OPTIX on GPU 1 (the idle A6000)
- 256 samples at 1024² resolution → ~5-8 minutes for 4 maps × 3 objects
- Output PNGs go under `ozu-test/room-1-textures/<slug>/`

For object passes where the SOURCE MESH itself must come from Blender (cloth-sim, sculpting), the path is glTF mesh-export + runtime mesh-load. This is currently parked behind the GLTFLoader bundle issue.

## Architecture and design notes

### The complete room-1 standalone sandbox pipeline (every step)

This is the precise sequence that took room-1 from blueprint to high-quality render. Each subsection is a re-usable building block. The numbered steps below define the order of construction inside the `registerScene('room-N', { build: function(S) { ... } })` block.

**STEP 0 — Outside the scene block (one-time setup, shared by all scenes).**
- 0.1 Vendor three.js r184 minimal bundle to `vendor/three/build/three.module.min.js` (already done).
- 0.2 Vendor HDRLoader to `vendor/three/examples/jsm/loaders/HDRLoader.js` (already done).
- 0.3 Vendor RoundedBoxGeometry to `vendor/three/examples/jsm/geometries/RoundedBoxGeometry.js` (already done).
- 0.4 Importmap entry `"three": "./vendor/three/build/three.module.min.js"` in `ozu-test.html:383-388`.
- 0.5 ESM imports in `<script type="module">`: `import * as THREE from 'three'`, `import { HDRLoader } from ...`, `import { RoundedBoxGeometry } from ...`.
- 0.6 Top-level renderer: `new THREE.WebGLRenderer({antialias: true})`, ACES filmic tone mapping, srgb output.
- 0.7 Top-level HDRI loader: `new HDRLoader().load('ozu-test/hdri/kloppenheim_06_puresky_2k.hdr', ...)` → PMREMGenerator → `_room1EnvMap` (cached, applied as `scene.environment` per-scene in `switchScene`).
- 0.8 Top-level `pbrMat` helper if not already present (loads diff/normal/rough from a slug).

**STEP 1 — Scene registration.**
```js
registerScene('room-N', {
  orbitPos: new THREE.Vector3(<corner-camera-pos>),
  orbitTarget: new THREE.Vector3(<room-center>),
  fpStart: new THREE.Vector3(<room-center-eye-level>),

  rooms: [],                     // no AABB collision boxes in standalone sandbox
  build: function(S) { /* steps 2-N */ }
});
```

Locally-scoped — these don't leak between scenes. Numbers come from the photo audit, not the blueprint.

**STEP 3 — Geometry primitive helpers (inside `build`).**
- `function box(w, h, d, cx, cy, cz, color, extra={})` — single-mesh `BoxGeometry` + `MeshStandardMaterial`.
- `function aabb(r, color, extra)` — `box(r.x2-r.x1, ..., (r.x1+r.x2)/2, ...)`.
- `function feat(axis, innerCoord, inward, dims, mat)` — wall-mounted feature box (window glass, panel, etc.). The "inner face sits BIAS away from the wall, rest of FEAT thickness extends INTO the room" pattern. Critical for avoiding WebGPU z-fighting bands.
- `function winFrame(axis, innerCoord, inward, a1, a2, b1, b2, mat)` — 4 trim strips (top, bottom, left, right) around a window opening.
- `function pbrMatR1(slug, repU, repV, extras={})` — loads diff/normal/rough textures from `ozu-test/room-1-textures/<slug>/`, sets sRGBColorSpace on diffuse, ClampToEdge / Repeat as appropriate, max anisotropy.
- `function applyClothShader(material, sheenColor, sheenStrength)` — manual `onBeforeCompile` fresnel patch for cloth edge highlights. NOTE: r155+ has native `MeshPhysicalMaterial.sheen`, prefer that for new cloth.
- `function applyFabricWeave(material, repeatU, repeatV, scale)` — clones a procedural weave normal map (Canvas2D `fabricNormalTex`), sets repeat + anisotropy.
- `function pipeFrame(panel, topRail, botRail, nBars)` — iron-frame footboard/sub-frame (cylinders + collar bands + ball cap).
- `function setUVsByLocal(geometry, uvFn, mainAxis)` — overrides BufferGeometry UV attribute so AO map wraps in world-aligned coordinates matching the bake script's parametric layout.

**STEP 4 — Procedural textures.**
- 4.1 Whitewashed plank ceiling: 1024² CanvasTexture with 9 procedurally-shaded planks, knots, grain noise, plank seam lines. Repeat (1.5, 1.5). `generateMipmaps: false; minFilter = LinearFilter` (WebGPU has mipmap moiré on high-frequency procedural textures).
- 4.2 Fabric weave normal map: 64² CanvasTexture with checkerboard alternating warp/weft sine-bump normals. Reused via clone + setRepeat for each cloth surface.

**STEP 5 — Floor + ceiling.**

```js
const floor = new THREE.Mesh(new THREE.BoxGeometry(RW, 0.02, RD), matFloorR1);   // PBR wood
floor.position.set(RW/2, -0.01, RD/2);
const ceil  = new THREE.Mesh(new THREE.BoxGeometry(RW, 0.02, RD), matCeilR1);    // procedural whitewashed plank
ceil.position.set(RW/2, RH + 0.01, RD/2);
```

**STEP 6 — Walls with baked AO + window cutouts.**
- 6.1 Define wall materials: `wallPaintArgs = { color: 0x<RoomPaintHex>, toneMapped: false }`. Crucial: `MeshBasicMaterial` + `toneMapped: false` so paint reads as the LITERAL color, no light-direction tint. The four walls use four DIFFERENT material instances (`matWall_x0`, `matWall_xW`, `matWall_z0`, `matWall_zD`) so each can have its own AO map.
- 6.2 `(function attachWallAO() { ... })()` — load `wall_x0.png`, `wall_xW.png`, `wall_z0.png`, `wall_zD.png` from `room-1-textures/wall_ao/`. `colorSpace = NoColorSpace` (AO is linear data, not sRGB). `generateMipmaps: false; min/magFilter = LinearFilter; wrapS/T = ClampToEdgeWrapping`.
- 6.3 Two walls as `BoxGeometry` (entrance-wall x=0, opposite ac/window walls): `wA, wC`. The other two walls (window-wall x=W, ac-wall z=D) need WINDOW CUTOUTS, so they're `ShapeGeometry` with a rectangular hole (`wallShapeWithHole(wallW, wallH, hx1, hy1, hx2, hy2)`).
- 6.4 `setUVsByLocal` override for each wall mesh so AO map wraps in world-aligned coordinates matching the bake's parametric layout. Crucial — without this, default BoxGeometry UVs are wrong on the thickness-side faces, and ShapeGeometry default UVs are raw shape coords (0..RD, 0..RH) not normalized — both cause visible texture errors on WebGPU.
- 6.5 Wall AO bake: run `ozu-test/bake/bake_room1_ao.py` with `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 ~/blender/blender-5.1.1-linux-x64/blender --background --python <path>`. Cycles + OPTIX, 256 samples, 1024² per surface, AO distance 0.6 m, margin 8 px.

**STEP 7 — Windows (glass + frosted) + frames + cutout matching.**
- 7.1 Refractive glass material: `MeshPhysicalMaterial({color, transparent:true, opacity:1, transmission:1, ior:1.52, thickness:0.02, roughness:0.04, metalness:0})`. Pickups env map automatically once `scene.environment` is set.
- 7.2 Frosted glass material: `MeshPhysicalMaterial({color, transparent:true, transmission:1, roughness:0.65, thickness:0.04, ior:1.52, attenuationColor, attenuationDistance:1.5})`. r184's transmission blur via `transmissionMap` roughness sampling gives true etched-glass diffusion.
- 7.3 Sliding window: `feat('x', RW, -1, { a1, a2, b1, b2 }, matGlassR1)` for the pane, `winFrame('x', RW, -1, a1, a2, b1, b2, matFrameR1)` for the 4 trim strips, plus a central vertical mullion `feat(...)` for the hikichigai meeting rail, plus a small chrome lever handle `MeshStandardMaterial({color:0xcfcfcf, metalness:0.95, roughness:0.2})` (3 small box meshes: escutcheon stem lever).
- 7.4 Frosted privacy window (ac-wall): `feat('z', RD, -1, {...}, matFrosted)` + `winFrame('z', RD, -1, ...)`.

**STEP 8 — Trim (baseboards + crown molding).**
- 8.1 Baseboard: 4 thin box meshes (one per wall) at floor level, 50 mm tall × 10 mm proud × full wall length. Nudged 1 mm INWARD from the wall surface (`ZF = 0.001`) so they don't share a plane with the wall's inside face (prevents WebGPU z-fight band).
- 8.2 Crown molding: 4 thin box meshes at ceiling level, 60 mm tall × 20 mm proud × full wall length. Same inward nudge.

**STEP 9 — Curtains (outer drape + inner sheer).**
- 9.1 Outer drape: a procedural "flutedColumn" function: `CylinderGeometry(rTop, rBot, height, 64, 1, false)` whose vertices are radially-displaced by a multi-octave sine pattern (8 vertical pleats, 25 mm deep, ±15% per-pleat jitter so they're not lathed-perfect). One column per drape bunch, with a TorusGeometry tieback ring at the tie height.
- 9.2 Inner sheer: PlaneGeometry covering ONLY the window opening (y = sill - 5 cm to sill + window_height + 5 cm, z = window_z_extent). Translucent (`opacity: 0.55`), `applyClothShader + applyFabricWeave`, double-sided, `depthWrite: false`. 32 width segments with sine-displaced vertex z for gather ripples.
- 9.3 Curtain bunch hem: respects furniture below. If bed/desk/shelf is in the column path, hem at "just above the highest furniture surface in that x/z column". NOT floor.

**STEP 10 — Door + casing.**
- 10.1 Doorway-fill box at the wall position with the doorway opening's interior (shrunk 5 mm each side from the actual door panel coords to avoid coplanar z-fight).
- 10.2 Flush trim: 3 thin strips (top header, left jamb, right jamb) at 20 mm wide × 5 mm proud × off-white. NOT proud architrave (real residential JP doors have flush trim).
- 10.3 Door panel: flat `box(0.8, 2.0, 0.04, ...)` white slab.
- 10.4 Three hinge cylinders on the hinge side (`y` axis aligned, `radius=0.012, length=0.07`).
- 10.5 Lever handle on the latch side: chrome `MeshStandardMaterial`, escutcheon plate (BoxGeometry) flush on door face, cylindrical stem perpendicular, horizontal lever bar parallel to door face extending toward the hinge side.

**STEP 11 — Closet (cabinet-wall side).**

- 11.2 Centre seam: thin slab via `feat(...)` with a darker grey `matSeam` material.

- 15.2 `DirectionalLight(0xfff4e0, 0.8)` positioned outside the window, aiming into the room. `castShadow = true`, `shadow.mapSize.set(4096, 4096)`, `shadow.camera.{left,right,top,bottom} = ±3`, `shadow.camera.near = 0.1`, `shadow.camera.far = 12`, `shadow.bias = -0.0005`, `shadow.normalBias = 0.02`, `shadow.radius = 4` (narrower than the thinnest geometry so shadows don't bleed through bedposts/mullion).

Oriented along the DirectionalLight's ray direction via `quaternion.setFromUnitVectors`.
- 15.5 Fill `DirectionalLight(0xede8df, 0.22)` from the cabinet-wall side — fills dark corners without doubling the cast shadow. Near-neutral very-slightly-warm; was previously cool sky-tint but that shifted the wall colour off the literal paint hex.
- 15.6 `S.group.traverse(obj => { ... })` — enable `castShadow` + `receiveShadow` on every mesh, EXCEPT: glass (`material.transmission > 0` skips casting), backdrops (`material.isMeshBasicMaterial` skips both), walls + ceiling (set to `castShadow=false, receiveShadow=false` so they don't pick up shadows of interior furniture and project them onto the outside faces — that would make the room look like an X-ray box from outside).
