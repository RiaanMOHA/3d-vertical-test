### 3. PIL `cannot write mode RGBA as JPEG`

**Root cause:** PNG screenshots are RGBA; JPEG doesn't support alpha.
**Fix:** `img = Image.open(src).convert("RGB")` before crop+save.

**Symptom:** Cropped image still showed a thin "Mar 2026" header banner top-left, the mini-map bottom-left, and the "Google Maps" copyright strip.
**Fix:** Tightened crop bounds from (900, 200, 2900, 1450) → (950, 260, 2890, 1270), losing about 60 px on each edge. Final 1940×1010 was clean.

### 5. World-positioned plane invisible from default orbit
**Symptom:** Plane at `(RW+8, 1.3, 1.0)` with normal facing -x — placed where the photo geographically belongs, but the default orbit camera at `(6.5, 4.5, 6.5)` looks toward `(1.35, 1.0, 1.35)` (view direction roughly -x/-z), so the plane sits *behind* the camera and never enters frame.
**Root cause:** A rectilinear photo on a flat plane has a front face only. The previous HDRI worked because three.js renders an equirectangular HDRI as a sphere at infinity, visible around the room silhouette from any orbit angle.
**Resolution:** Switched to `scene.background = Texture` (screen-fitted 2D). Loses parallax/world-alignment but is always visible. Documented the tradeoff in code comments and to the user.

### 6. Conceptual: room is fully enclosed → can't see "through" the sliding window from outside
The default orbit is *outside* the room on +x +z. Looking through the sliding window into the room, the line of sight hits the **closet-wall at x=0 (opaque BoxGeometry)** and stops. So the HDRI was never really visible *through* the window from the default angle — it was visible *around* the room silhouette. My earlier session text ("look out through the sliding window") was misleading; what actually happens is the backdrop fills the negative space around the room.

## Architecture and design notes

### Backdrop technique tradeoff (this is the key architectural decision of the session)

| Approach | Visibility | Camera tracking | Implementation cost | Quality vs photo source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Equirectangular HDRI** (original) | All angles, around room silhouette | Full 360° world-aligned | None (HDRLoader) | Requires equirectangular source |
| **`scene.background` = 2D Texture** (chosen) | All angles, screen-fitted | None — glued to screen | Trivial (TextureLoader swap) | Works with rectilinear photos |
| **World-positioned plane** | Only when camera is on plane's normal side and view is unblocked | Parallax + world-aligned | Moderate (placement, sizing, orbit choreography) | Works with rectilinear photos |
| **Cube/sphere with photo on one face** | All angles, but photo only occupies one direction | World-aligned | Moderate | Other faces need fill (sky color or stitched neighbours) |
| **Stitched equirectangular** | All angles, around room silhouette | Full 360° world-aligned | Complex (panorama stitching pipeline) | Best — matches HDRI behaviour with real-location source |

The chosen approach is the **minimum-change** option: swap the HDRLoader for TextureLoader, keep all the surrounding scene.background plumbing identical. If the user wants better fidelity (parallax or 360°), that's a future iteration.

### Constraint: room walls fully enclose the room
- All four walls (closet, entrance, window, ac) reach floor to near-ceiling height. The two with windows have rectangular holes; the other two are solid `BoxGeometry`.
- With `noFirstPerson: true`, the orbit camera is always outside the room. From outside, looking *through* a window into the room, the line of sight hits the opposite opaque wall — so the backdrop is **never visible through a window from the default orbit angle**. It's only visible *around the room silhouette*.
- This means "look out through the sliding window" is more aspirational than literal — the actual user experience is "see the backdrop in the negative space around the room." Future sessions should not over-specify "through the window" geometry; the visual effect is silhouette backdrop, not portal view.

### Coordinate frames in room-1
- Room interior: x ∈ [0, RW=2.7], y ∈ [0, RH=2.5], z ∈ [0, RD=2.7]
- Window-wall at x=RW; sliding-window opening at z=[0.405, 1.595], y=[0.85, 1.75] — outward face is +x
- AC-wall at z=RD; frosted-window opening at x=[0.30, 0.56], y=[0.9, 1.9] — outward face is +z
- Default orbit: pos (6.5, 4.5, 6.5), target (1.35, 1.0, 1.35) — view direction roughly (-1, -0.5, -1) normalised, sphere radius ~8 m around target
