## Problems encountered and how they were solved

### Problem 1: Stairs handrail material misidentified

- **Symptom.** Prior session's `stairs/room-map.md` claimed handrail was "light maple/birch wood with brushed-chrome brackets and a chrome elbow connector". Photo evidence (sampled this session) showed a uniform-paint cylindrical rail with no wood grain.
- **Root cause.** Prior session inferred materials from limited photo sampling. The painted handrail in stairs-7 was misread as "light wood" (since the cream/off-white paint can look like pale wood at a glance). The "chrome elbow connector" was misread from the same photo — the connector is also painted to match the rail.
- **Fix.** Re-sampled stairs-4 (clean handrail close-up on a straight section) which clearly shows the uniform white paint. Re-read stairs-7 and confirmed the elbow joint is also painted, not chrome. Updated all material references in the stairs map.
- **Lesson.** When inferring material from a photo, look for grain (wood) vs uniform paint vs metallic finish vs textured surface. A close-up sampling of a featureless straight section is more diagnostic than a corner shot.

### Problem 2: Stairs nosing strips misidentified

- **Symptom.** Prior session's stairs map said "chrome anti-slip strip on each tread". Photo evidence (stairs-10, stairs-17, stairs-27) showed two parallel DARK lines on each nosing.
- **Root cause.** Prior session described the visible feature as "chrome" without close inspection. The dark inlay strips look like dark walnut inlay or rubberised anti-slip grooves, NOT metal/chrome.
- **Fix.** Updated all references to "dark inlay anti-slip strips (two parallel grooves) per tread nosing".
- **Lesson.** Light vs dark anti-slip strips are diagnostic for the safety strip material. When sampling tread photos, look at the nosing edge profile carefully — chrome would reflect, inlay/rubberised wouldn't.

### Problem 3: Logical contradiction in stairs map "assumed" section after material fixes

For folders with letter-coded sub-folders (a/b/c/d), camera-position assignment follows this method:

### Triple-swap rename pattern

When 3 identifiers need to swap among themselves (e.g., room1 → room-3, room2 → room-1, room3 → room-2), use 3 sequential replace_alls in order:
1. Replace `room2` → `room-1` (target name doesn't exist as substring of source; "room1" still untouched)
2. Replace `room3` → `room-2` (same — "room1" still untouched)
3. Replace `room1` → `room-3` (last — by now no other source matches exist)

When a user invokes a memory rule (this session: `feedback_exact_folder_names.md` triggered by the screenshot of "bedroom" chip), the response should be:

2. Sweep ALL files (not just the immediate file) for the rule violation
